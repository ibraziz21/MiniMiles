# Video 01 — First post on TikTok + YouTube Shorts

**Concept:** #1 — "You spend KES 20,000 a month on the usual things. Here's what that should be earning you."
**Length:** 40–45 sec
**Pillar:** More For Your Money
**Goal of this video:** establish the frame (same spend, more value) and plant one habit. Not to sell Akiba.

---

## Why this is the first video, not an intro video

Resist the urge to announce yourself. Nobody on a cold TikTok feed is looking for a founder introduction, and an "hi, I'm starting a channel" video gives the algorithm nothing to work with — no retention, no shares, no comments.

More importantly: this video is what people will scroll back to in six months when they discover you. Make video one look like video fifty. Your identity goes in the last eight seconds, where it reads as credibility instead of a pitch.

The origin story belongs on YouTube long-form (see bottom of this doc).

---

### What changed and why

You were right that the old ending was broken. It criticised single-store cards and then told the viewer to go looking for single-store rewards — the same dead end, restated. Worse, it left the video as a complaint with no resolution.

The fix is a **reframe before the solution**: *"shida si ya stores, shida ni kila mtu anabuild yake solo."* That single line moves the villain from merchants to fragmentation — which matters commercially, because you need those merchants to sign, and a video that makes them the bad guy poisons your own pipeline. Then the payoff earns its place: not "check harder," but *account moja, everything pools, kitu unaeza actually tumia.*

Note the emphasis on **redeemable**. "Si points zinakaa hapo till zinaexpire" is the real consumer pain and the sharpest differentiator you have — points people can't spend are the reason nobody trusts loyalty programs. Say it in every version of this pitch.

**Two notes on your draft:**

- **"Unaweza kumbuka last time ulitumia points za supermarket?"** is the best line in the script — rhetorical, everyone's answer is *no*, and it proves the problem without you arguing it. It now sits right before the reframe so it does the pivoting. Strong enough to be the hook too; see variant 2.
- **"which only accumulate on purchases in the same store"** was the one spot where the flow stiffened into written English. `"inaaccumulate only kwa store hiyo"` holds the register.

## Hook variants to test

Film all four openings in the same sitting. Five seconds each, and the hook is the entire ballgame.

1. **(Default)** "Many young professionals in Nairobi spend at least 20K a month on shopping na everyday expenses."
2. **(Strong contender)** "Unaweza kumbuka last time ulitumia points za supermarket? Exactly. Hiyo ni shida."
3. "Airtime, coffee, groceries, transport, subscriptions. Si luxury — lakini ni hii mob." *(number appears)*
4. "Hii pesa unaspend kila mwezi inaenda either way. Swali ni moja tu."

Variant 2 opens on a question the viewer answers in their head before they can swipe — that's the mechanic you want. Run 1 on TikTok and 2 on Shorts and let the retention curves settle it.

## Production notes

- **Face to camera, phone, vertical.** Natural light — window on your face, not behind you.
- **Clip-on mic.** Audio is the difference between this landing and dying at second three.
- **No intro, no greeting, no outro.** Start on the first word of the hook. Cut the moment you finish the last one.
- **Hard cut on every line.** Small jump cuts keep short-form retention up; dead air kills it.
- **On-screen text throughout.** Assume sound-off viewing. Keep text large, high-contrast, upper-middle third — away from the caption overlay at the bottom.
- **Fix the auto-captions.** CapCut will mangle Sheng and Swahili badly. Budget five minutes to correct them — wrong captions on your own language reads as careless to exactly the audience you're courting.
- **One take per line.** If a line needs four takes, the line is wrong, not your delivery. Rewrite it shorter.
- **Watermark:** if reposting to Shorts, remove the TikTok watermark first — YouTube throttles it.

## On the ask

The video now delivers the **solution message** — one account, value everywhere, actually redeemable. That's different from delivering a **download ask**, and video one should do the first without the second.

The reason isn't modesty, it's sequencing. You're still refining PMF and onboarding founding merchants. Cold traffic arriving at an early product churns once and doesn't come back, and you'd have spent your best video buying users you can't yet keep. Ask for a **follow** instead — it costs the viewer nothing, and it's the right ask for a build-in-public channel: *stay and watch this get made.* Convert that audience to signups later, when the merchant network is dense enough that the app delivers on the promise this video makes.

So:

- **Do** land the concept clearly: account moja, everywhere, rewards unaeza tumia.
- **Do** end on "follow" — it's the honest ask right now.
- **Don't** say "download Akiba" or add a link sticker in video one. Bio only.
- **Don't** quote a reward percentage or a specific benefit. Vague-and-true beats specific-and-provisional.
- **Don't** explain the business model, the merchant side, or the roadmap. One clause of identity is enough — curiosity outperforms explanation.

## The YouTube companion (same week)

Long-form #1 — **"Why I'm building a rewards network in Kenya."** 9–12 min. This is the one you pin and the one you send investors.

Rough beats:

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:45)** — the specific moment you noticed the problem. A concrete scene, not a thesis. No intro card before this.
2. **The consumer problem (0:45–3:00)** — dozens of disconnected loyalty programs, none of them worth using. Show the pile.
3. **The merchant problem (3:00–5:30)** — small businesses can't build retention infrastructure, and acquisition is expensive. Use a real merchant conversation if you have one on camera.
4. **The insight (5:30–7:00)** — both sides fail for the same reason: everyone is building loyalty alone. One shared network fixes both.
5. **What Akiba is (7:00–9:00)** — Akiba Pass, the merchant side, actual screens. Show, don't describe.
6. **Where we actually are (9:00–11:00)** — early, onboarding founding merchants, running pilots. Say it plainly. Understatement is what makes the rest credible.
7. **What this channel is (11:00–end)** — you'll document it weekly, wins and misses. One sentence, then stop.

**Title matters more than the video.** Write it before you shoot. Test against: *"Why I'm building a rewards network in Kenya"* / *"I'm building the app Kenya's shops should have built together"* / *"Kenya has a loyalty problem. I quit infrastructure to fix it."*
