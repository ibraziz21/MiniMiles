# Longform 01 — The Content Base

**Working title:** Why I'm building a rewards network in Kenya
**Runtime:** 35–45 min recorded, cut to ~30 for YouTube
**Output:** 1 pinned YouTube video + 10 shorts = roughly 3 weeks of TikTok
**Time cost:** ~90 min to record, ~3 hrs to harvest. Fits the 5-hour week.

---

## Why this is the better first move

Two real advantages over batching five shorts:

**It's easier.** Talking for 40 minutes about something you know is a far lower bar than delivering five ice-cold 40-second hooks with no camera reps. You'll be bad at hooks for about three weeks. You are already good at explaining Akiba.

**It's the asset you actually need.** The pinned longform is what you send investors and what a merchant watches before taking your call. Shorts get you found; this is what makes people take you seriously once they do.

And the shorts come out of it for free — which is the part that only works if you structure it right.

## Record it, don't livestream it — at least this one

Livestreaming to an empty room is demoralising, gives you no second takes, and pins your worst-quality video at the top of a brand-new channel. Record this one. Once you've got an audience and a few reps, live Q&A becomes genuinely good content — and by then you'll have questions worth answering live.

Recording as if it were live is fine. One take, minimal editing, don't chase polish.

---

## The one rule that makes this work

**Speak in self-contained segments.**

A 40-minute ramble is unclippable — every good line depends on something you said eight minutes earlier, so nothing survives extraction. The fix is to treat this as **ten separate mini-talks that happen to be recorded back to back.**

For every segment:

1. **Restate the question before answering it.** Not "yeah so about that…" but "Why does every shop in Kenya build its own loyalty program?" The clip needs to make sense with no setup.
2. **Open with the point, not a warm-up.** Land the conclusion in the first sentence, then explain. Warm-up sentences are what make clips unusable.
3. **Never reference earlier segments.** Kill "like I said before," "as I mentioned," "going back to." Each one destroys a clip.
4. **Say numbers out loud and clearly.** "Twenty thousand shillings a month" — not "that number."
5. **Pause three full seconds between segments.** Silence gives you clean cut points and saves you an hour in the edit.
6. **Same register as the shorts.** Code-switch naturally. Don't shift into corporate English because the camera's been on for 20 minutes.

## Get someone to interview you

Biggest single upgrade available: hand the ten questions below to your co-founder or a friend and have them ask you, off-camera, out of frame.

You'll be looser, less monotone, and you'll answer instead of perform. First-time monologues almost always sound like a pitch; conversations sound like a person. Just brief them to stay silent while you talk — their "mm-hmm" over your best line costs you that clip.

## Run sheet

Each row is a segment. Answer it fully, then pause and move on. Aim 3–4 min each.

| # | Question to answer on camera | Target | Yields short |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What does a normal month actually cost a young professional in Nairobi?" | 3 min | **20K a month na si luxury** (already scripted) |
| 2 | "When did you last use points from a supermarket?" | 3 min | **Points za supermarket… za mwisho lini?** |
| 3 | "Why does every business in Kenya build its own loyalty program?" | 4 min | **Shida si ya stores — ni kila mtu anabuild solo** |
| **3b** | **"Why do rewards actually change what people do?"** | **5 min** | **Bonga points** · **discount vs loop** · **the math forbids it** — see `segment-why-incentives-matter.md` |
| 4 | "What do small businesses in Kenya actually struggle with — getting customers, or keeping them?" | 4 min | **Acquisition vs retention** (merchant pillar) |
| 5 | "What is one repeat customer worth to a shop over a year?" | 3 min | **The retention math** (merchant pillar) |
| 6 | "Why did you leave blockchain infrastructure to build this?" | 4 min | **Personal / origin** |
| 7 | "What is Akiba, concretely? Show me." | 5 min | **Product walkthrough** — screen recording, not talking head |
| 8 | "Where are you actually today? Be honest." | 3 min | **Build in public — the real status** |
| 9 | "What have you gotten wrong so far?" | 4 min | **Build in public — strongest clip in the video** |
| 10 | "What does Kenyan commerce look like in ten years if this works?" | 4 min | **The vision** |

**Segment 3b is the thesis.** It's the only segment worth genuinely preparing rather than winging, because it's what turns "one account is convenient" into "fragmentation is why loyalty in Kenya has never worked." Full brief in `segment-why-incentives-matter.md`.

**Segment 9 is the one to prepare properly.** "What I got wrong" outperforms everything else on both platforms and is the single most credible thing a founder can say publicly. Pick two specific failures with specific details — a feature nobody used, a merchant who walked. Vague humility reads as false; specific failure reads as competence.

**Segment 7 needs a screen recording.** Capture Akiba Pass on your phone separately and cut it in. Don't describe the product while sitting in a chair — show it.

---

## Order matters

Record **6 first, then 1–5, then 7–10.**

Segment 6 is your origin story — you've told it a hundred times, so it warms you up on autopilot. By segment 1 you'll sound like yourself. Never open a recording session with your hardest segment.

In the **published edit**, reorder to: 1 → 2 → 3 → 3b → 6 → 4 → 5 → 7 → 8 → 9 → 10. Problem the viewer feels, then why it exists, then why you, then the merchant side, then the product, then the honest state of things.

---

## Setup

- **Phone, horizontal, on a tripod, eye level.** Horizontal for YouTube — you'll crop to vertical for the shorts, so frame yourself slightly off-centre with headroom to spare.
- **Window on your face.** Not behind you. Record mid-morning or late afternoon.
- **Clip-on mic, and test 30 seconds of it before you commit 40 minutes.** Checking audio afterwards is how people lose a whole session.
- **Somewhere that says something.** Office, a merchant's shop, anywhere with texture. Not a blank wall.
- **Water in frame is fine.** Forty minutes is a long time to talk.
- **Don't memorise anything.** The ten questions are on a sheet off-camera. That's the whole prep.

---

## Harvest workflow

Do this the day after recording, not the same day — you'll cut more ruthlessly with fresh ears.

1. **Watch it once at 1.5x** with a notepad. Timestamp every moment you'd actually send to a friend. Expect 10–14 of them.
2. **Cut the YouTube version.** Trim dead air, drop your two weakest segments, add chapter markers (chapters matter — people will jump straight to the product and the vision). Aim ~30 min.
3. **Pull the shorts.** Crop vertical, top-and-tail hard, add on-screen text. Each one has to open on a hook — if your best line is 20 seconds into the segment, **start the clip there** and throw away the setup.
4. **Auto-caption, then fix them.** CapCut mangles Sheng. Budget five minutes per clip.
5. **Schedule 4 per week.** Post the ones you're least sure about first — the honest failure clips and the sharp opinion clips usually beat the polished explanation ones, and you want that data early.

**Keep the offcuts.** Lines that don't fit a clip this month become next month's videos. This one recording should carry you three weeks minimum.

## Publishing order

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Record + 2 | Publish shorts from segments 1 and 2 |
| Record + 4 | Publish the YouTube longform. Pin it. |
| Record + 5 | Short from segment 9 (what I got wrong) — your best clip, once the channel has a pulse |
| Then | 4 shorts/week from the remaining segments |

Shorts go out **before** the longform. They pull people toward a video that's already sitting there waiting, instead of publishing your best asset into an empty room.

## Title and thumbnail

Write the title before you record — it keeps you on message for 40 minutes.

Candidates:

- *Why I'm building a rewards network in Kenya*
- *Kenya has a loyalty problem. I left infrastructure to fix it.*
- *Every Kenyan shop built its own loyalty program. That's the problem.*
- *20K a month, and none of it comes back*

Last one is the strongest for a cold audience — it's the consumer hook, not the founder hook. First one is the strongest for the investor who's already looking you up. If you want both, use the consumer title and put the founder framing in the first line of the description.

**Thumbnail:** your face, one short phrase, high contrast. Three words maximum. *"None of it comes back"* works.
